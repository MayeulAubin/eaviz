About

Eä is a dark-matter simulation of the Universe as an observer sees it: a light-cone 5.7 Gpc/h deep (out to redshift 6, 12.8 billion years back) around one observer, computed with the sCOLA method in 388,744 tiles on the TGCC supercomputer. Every point of light here is a dark-matter halo, the home of a galaxy; there are no stars or gas.

Sky shows what the observer sees: halos are as bright as their mass and distance make them, coloured by their velocity dispersion (bluer = more massive) and redshift (redder = farther). Pre-rendered uses the film's images; Live draws the 3.9 million brightest halos here, with every option. Fly lets you travel through the light-cone: the farther you go, the older the Universe you see.

The Milky Way is a real all-sky map, added for scale; the CMB is a simulated one with Eä's cosmology. The dark matter is the simulated density, out to 1.5 Gpc/h. Lensing maps are computed from the simulation itself (Born approximation); strong lenses are isothermal spheres set by each cluster's velocity dispersion.

Visualisation code: eaviz (GPLv3).
